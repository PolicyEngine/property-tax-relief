Sources and methods.

The legal inventory and model audit answer different questions: what an official source describes, which implementation exists, and whether a calculation reproduces the rule.

National calculation release remains closed. The draft reports program rules and source-level implementation presence. It contains no validated national household benefit estimates.

Research scope

The inventory covers all 50 states and the District of Columbia. It focuses on statewide property-tax credits, circuit breakers, rebates and renter credits. It includes selected exemptions, deductions, deferrals, tax limits and local options to explain relief outside the credit and rebate comparison. Relief can reduce a property-tax bill or income-tax liability, or arrive as a separate payment.

Each profile records a 2025 program reference and a source-check date of November 13, 2026. The program-year field identifies differences among assessment, income, tax-payment, filing and benefit-payment years. The profile includes later changes where research identified them. A source-check date does not establish that every provision has received a legal audit.

Official agency guidance, forms and statutes supply the program descriptions. “No statewide credit identified” describes the research result, not proof that no relief exists. The inventory does not enumerate every local program, military benefit, disaster provision or parcel-specific exemption. Housing tenure refers to ownership or rental of the dwelling. Owners of a dwelling on leased land appear under homeowners.

Model-presence audit

The audit reads declared variable classes in the pinned release and compares them with the inventory's model-variable references. It requires a formula or declarative calculation available by 2025; input-only variables and formulas that begin later enter the gap list. It does not execute formulas, establish legal equivalence or verify all eligibility conditions. Newer canonical source presence is recorded separately.

Household-calculation requirements

Every displayed household amount must come from an actual PolicyEngine run for that scenario. A usable credit requires the correct income definition, household composition, age, disability status, housing expenses, prior-year facts and filing assumptions. Nonrefundable credits also require tax-liability calculations. Program interactions need explicit checks.

Source-derived examples, boundary tests, accounting and cap invariants, and comparison with official schedules must precede publication of national totals. A missing implementation must never appear as a $0 benefit. A statutory percentage of rent is a calculation input, not measured tax incidence. Eligibility estimates do not establish take-up or fiscal expenditure.

Rent and property taxes across states

State income-tax credits, deductions and subtractions for rent and property taxes sit alongside separate rebates and property-tax bill programs. This review records selected provisions in all 50 states and the District of Columbia, using program-specific 2025 reference periods and official sources checked on November 13–14, 2026.

The 77 statewide records in the main comparison describe program rules; validated national household calculations remain outstanding. The systematic audit of deductions, subtractions and itemization rules remains open. This inventory does not establish exhaustive program coverage.

Program names conceal different mechanisms

A threshold circuit breaker links a credit to housing costs above a specified share of program income. Other credits reduce tax liability, deductions and subtractions reduce taxable income, and rebates may arrive as separate payments. Exemptions, tax limits and repayable deferrals have their own effects. The inventory records the mechanism separately from the program name.

North Carolina calls one program a circuit breaker but defers qualifying property taxes. California provides a flat, nonrefundable renter credit. Massachusetts offers qualifying seniors a refundable credit based on housing expenses and income. A comparison should retain these distinctions.

Separate the income, tax and payment years

A 2025 reference can describe income, an assessment, property taxes paid, rent paid, or an application. Those events need separate labels. Each profile records the relevant dates and later changes.

Minnesota increased its 2025 homeowner refund after a 2026 law change. New Jersey's current Stay NJ guidance for the 2025 tax year describes payments in 2027 and income-tiered annualized caps. Reproducing an older schedule would miss those revisions.

Account for overlapping relief

Some programs require a choice; others reduce an existing tax bill or offset another benefit. The profiles record these interactions. Kansas offers alternative refund routes, New Jersey coordinates ANCHOR, Senior Freeze and Stay NJ, and Wisconsin shows some credits directly on property-tax bills. Adding published maxima without those rules would overstate relief.

Other research and coverage gaps

Existing inventories provide different views of rent and property taxes. Their dates, program definitions and geographic coverage differ, so their totals cannot be compared directly with this inventory's record count.

  • The Lincoln Institute's Significant Features of the Property Tax residential database supplies 2024 program records spanning credits, exemptions, assessment freezes, deferrals and local options. A record can describe an umbrella program or a subgroup.
  • ITEP's November 9, 2026 circuit-breaker report lists 47 program rows in Appendix B, with income limits described as current in July 2026 and some older source data. Its circuit-breaker definition includes some income-based exemptions.
  • AARP Foundation's Property Tax-Aide state guides update on a rolling basis and combine statewide programs with county variants. They do not supply a single comparable reference year or normalized national program total.
  • The Progress and Poverty Institute and Center for Land Economics mid-2026 Maine report examines Maine's Property Tax Fairness Credit, homestead exemption and state property-tax deferral. Its Maine focus does not constitute a national program inventory.

Current income-tax coverage includes selected renter credits, Massachusetts and Indiana rent deductions, and Indiana and New Jersey property-tax deductions. Separate records describe selected circuit breakers, rebates and bill credits. Exemptions, deferrals, assessment limits and local options remain contextual categories. State-return subtractions, itemization rules and federal-state conformity still require a systematic audit.

Cross-checks also identify programs that this draft has not yet enumerated, including Montana disabled-veteran assistance, Washington veteran-survivor grants, South Dakota's paraplegic tax reduction, Hawaii county circuit breakers, and Maine's homestead exemption and property-tax deferral. Some existing records group several qualifying categories. These differences prevent an exhaustive-coverage claim or a national completeness percentage.

Automatic credit for taxable real-estate parcels with a real-property improvement. The improvement establishes eligibility; total parcel value determines the capped base.

Who

2025 property-tax bills, payable 2026.

Nonrefundable: limited to applicable tax liability

Income definition. Not income-tested; household income is not used to calculate this bill credit.

Other income-tested relief · Statewide

Property Tax Refund Program

Income-tier refund using paid taxes, county median tax cap and asset/residency tests.

Who
Homeowners. No general age restriction.
Income limits
Up to 145% of applicable median income, with lower benefits above the 125% tier.
Maximum / measure
75% of eligible taxes paid, limited to half the county median residential tax bill; upper income tier receives a 35% reduction.
Reference period
2025 property taxes, refund application in 2026.
Payment mechanism
Refundable credit or cash rebate
Eligibility, claiming and calculation notes

Income definition. Total household income compared with the greater of county or state median household income.

How to claim. Annual application through Department of Revenue or official refund portal; supporting tax-payment and household-income records required.

  • Wyoming resident at least five years; primary residence.
  • For 2025, combined countable household assets must not exceed $169,866 times the number of adult household members, with program exclusions.
  • The asset test is waived when the person's tax liability is strictly greater than 10% of household income; equality does not trigger the waiver.
